There are three ways to get authentication data into **Corbado Observe** from a web frontend, and the native SDKs for iOS and Android. The web shapes are three shapes of one architecture: every integration has **signal sources** and **one mapping** that turns signals into Observe flows, decisions and subflows. The paths differ only in where the signals come from and who owns the mapping. All three feed the same project, the same session and the same data model.

| | **Autocapture** | **Data layer + mapping** | **Custom events** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Signals | Browser surfaces: network, WebAuthn, DOM | Screen offers, outcomes and validation results your app pushes, plus network and WebAuthn captured in your page | Tracker calls in your own code |
| Mapping | Built and maintained by Corbado, adjusted after your releases | One central module in Corbado's adapter structure | Spread across your components |
| App changes | None, ever | A stub, one data attribute per control, thin emitters and capture wiring | Tracker calls at every semantic point |
| Delivery | Corbado script tag (recommended) or self-hosted | Corbado script tag (recommended) or self-hosted | Bundled with your app |
| Recommended for | Fully managed, no source changes | Integrating from source | Single events and deliberate hand-written instrumentation |

## 1. Autocapture

<Info>
  **Recommended when you do not want to change your code at all.** Fully managed by Corbado: one script, no event tagging, nothing to maintain.
</Info>

You deploy a single script and Corbado maps your authentication journey from signals your frontend already emits: API calls, WebAuthn ceremonies, fields and their low-level events, existing component events. No event tagging, no change to your login flows and no instrumentation to maintain as your login evolves. Corbado owns the complete mapping and adjusts the tracking after larger releases of your login. You do not change code. This is why it is the fully managed option for large enterprises.

### 1.1 What Autocapture actually does

Autocapture is not a black box. Concretely, it does five things in the browser:

| Mechanism | What it means in practice |
| --- | --- |
| **Inspects the DOM tree** | Locates the elements your authentication runs through: identifier and password fields, OTP inputs, submit and method-selection buttons, including across shadow-DOM boundaries. Field semantics such as `autocomplete="new-password"` distinguish a "set new password" field from a login field. |
| **Listens to DOM events** | `focus`, `blur`, `input`, `click` and `submit` on those elements produce the low-level signals that show *how* a user moved through a screen, including hesitation, retries and abandoned fields. |
| **Observes the WebAuthn API** | The full lifecycle of `navigator.credentials.get()` and `create()`, including **silent cancellations** that produce no error and no backend request. |
| **Observes your auth network calls** | The start and outcome of requests to the endpoints Corbado is configured to watch, correlated by request ID. All other traffic is ignored. |
| **Follows in-app navigation** | Same-document (SPA) route changes, so a journey stays intact across a client-side router. |

Those raw signals are then mapped onto your actual flows, so which request means "identifier accepted" and which button means "user chose password over passkey". They arrive in Observe as a modeled journey.

Autocapture is built on [`@corbado/autocapture`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@corbado/autocapture), a dependency-free capture library designed to run inside a page it does not own and to fail closed when a browser surface cannot be instrumented safely.

<Note>
  Autocapture pairs the capture library with a mapping for **your** flows, which Corbado builds and keeps current. It is therefore set up together with Corbado during onboarding and is not self-serve. [Talk to us](mailto:support@corbado.com) to get started.
</Note>

## 2. Data layer + mapping

<Info>
  **Recommended when you integrate from source.** Like Autocapture it runs on one central mapping module that turns signals into Observe events. The difference: your app pushes the screen events, network and WebAuthn are captured in your page, and the mapping can live in your codebase.
</Info>

Your screens carry one data attribute per control, and your code pushes a few events into the **Observe data layer**, an in-page queue with the mechanics of an analytics data layer: a screen was presented with these options, an option was chosen, validation rejected a field. Network calls and WebAuthn ceremonies are captured automatically in your page by the same library Autocapture uses and pushed into the same queue. One central module, the **mapping**, takes the queue over and turns everything into Observe flows, decisions and subflows.

Your app keeps the stub, the tagged markup, the emitters and the capture wiring. They are your code and evolve with your screens. The mapping holds every Observe name, every flow boundary and every subflow step, and imports nothing from your app. This is why it can be replaced: host it yourself as your own package, or let Corbado serve it as a script tag, the recommended delivery, so that a tracking correction is live on the next page load without an app release. Because its input is a plain event array, tracking logic is testable by replaying recorded journeys.

See [Get started with the data layer](/corbado-observe/get-started/data-layer). The [agent skill](/corbado-observe/get-started/use-with-ai-agents) implements it.

## 3. Custom events

Custom events are explicit **Corbado Observe** events you emit from your own code, at the semantically correct points in your flows. You get exactly the granularity you define. This is the right path for single events and when a step's meaning must be fixed in code.

Every call site follows the taxonomy rules on its own, so the effort grows with the number of places you instrument. Every tracking correction is an app release, and there is no central place Corbado can review and improve with you. On web, prefer the data layer unless you deliberately want hand-written tracker calls.

See [Custom events](/corbado-observe/get-started/custom-events) to send your first one, and the [tracking reference](/corbado-observe/tracking/overview) for the full data model.

## 4. Native apps: iOS and Android

On iOS and Android the native SDKs take the role of the data layer and the capture together. The SDK captures what your code cannot see, such as the app's state churn around system sheets and biometrics, autofill and credential-manager evidence, device capability and delivery through a durable outbox. Your screens forward a few facts through typed hooks: the current screen, field lengths and focus, the ceremony result and the request result. One adapter module in your app turns those facts into flows, decisions and subflows, the same separation as the mapping on web.

Corbado calls this **Autocapture Light**: your app exposes facts, the mapping interprets them. The data layer on web is the same idea. On native there is no script-only mode, so the hooks are always part of the app and updates ship with your releases.

See [Get started on iOS and Android](/corbado-observe/get-started/native).

## 5. Where Autocapture stops

The boundary is not "simple flows vs. complex flows". It is **observability in the client**.

Autocapture can work with anything that is visible in the browser or reported back to it: rendered UI states, user interactions, WebAuthn ceremonies, navigation and the shape of requests and responses. From those signals Corbado's mapping derives the product meaning, including which method a user chose, whether an identifier was already known, whether a ceremony was a login or an enrollment and whether the user or the system started a step.

The data layer and custom events are for facts that never reach the browser, or that you want your app to state explicitly:

| Case | Example | Path |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The app knows what the browser only shows** | Which of three transiently painted password forms is the intended one, or why a field was rejected | Data layer |
| **The fact exists only in your backend** | A risk decision, a fraud score or a segment that influences the journey but is never rendered | Data layer, routed through your app as a request result or context |
| **Business context Observe cannot know** | Your own [tags](/corbado-observe/tracking/tags), [user references](/corbado-observe/tracking/user), experiments and conversions outside authentication | Data layer context, or custom events |
| **The fact exists only outside the browser** | A QR-code login where the scan happens in your mobile app | Custom events from the app |
| **Native apps** | iOS and Android have no DOM and no WebAuthn browser API to observe | The native SDKs, see section 4 |

<Note>
  Where an event needs to join a journey that started in the browser, it is correlated using an ID you already have, such as a challenge or transaction ID, otherwise the Observe session ID.
</Note>

## 6. Combining paths

Running paths together is a supported setup. Autocapture provides the baseline journey and you add data layer events or custom events only for the gaps that matter, including enrichment from your backend. Both web and native apps in one project is the normal enterprise setup: Autocapture or the data layer on web, the native SDKs in the apps, and [applications](/corbado-observe/tracking/applications) to keep the channels comparable.

All paths write into the same project, the same session and the same data model, so added events appear inside the same journey. Because the exact wiring depends on how the mapping is delivered, set a combined web setup up together with us. Do not add a second SDK instance to the page.

## 7. Choosing

| | **Autocapture** | **Data layer + mapping** | **Custom events** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Platforms | Web | Web | Web |
| Integration effort | **One line of code.** Corbado prepares the script for your flows. | A stub, tagged controls, thin emitters and capture wiring, typically **hours** with the agent skill | **1 to 3 days** for a typical login flow, plus your own testing |
| Time to first insight | Same day | Same day | As soon as your first instrumented flow ships |
| Semantics | Derived from observed signals | Stated by your app, translated centrally | Explicit: you define flow, touchpoint, spec type and decisions |
| Maintenance | Corbado maintains everything, including reconciling the tracking after your larger releases | Your emitters evolve with your code; the mapping is updated in one place, by you or by Corbado | Your team maintains events as flows change |
| Delivery | Corbado script tag (recommended) or self-hosted | Corbado script tag (recommended) or self-hosted | Bundled with your app |
| Testability | Corbado's recorded scenarios | Replay recorded data layer events against the mapping | Your own end-to-end runs |
| Coverage | Every flow the script sees | Every flow your app reports | Exactly the flows you instrument |

**What we recommend:**

- **Web, no code changes wanted:** Autocapture, fully managed by Corbado.
- **Web, integrating from source:** the data layer. Use custom events only when you deliberately want hand-written tracker calls or individual events.
- **iOS and Android:** the native SDKs, see [Get started on iOS and Android](/corbado-observe/get-started/native).
- **Both web and native apps:** Autocapture or the data layer on web **and** the native SDKs in the apps. One project holds all channels, and [applications](/corbado-observe/tracking/applications) keep them comparable.

<Tip>
  **Want to try one flow first?** [Tell us which flow](mailto:support@corbado.com) and we prepare an Autocapture script for it. You add one line, walk the flow and see the data. That is the fastest way to judge whether Observe fits before committing to a full integration.
</Tip>

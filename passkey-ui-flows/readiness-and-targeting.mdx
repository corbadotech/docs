Readiness and targeting logic selects the passkey flow that is most likely to work in the current context. It can use rules, first-party state, a service or a learned model. The benchmark checks the selected route, not the architecture or product name.

## Decision actions

Each readiness decision should result in one routing action that can be inspected. Later evidence can trigger another decision, so these actions are not mutually exclusive final outcomes:

-   **Attempt:** Start a credential ceremony when the evidence supports doing so without another first-party prompt.
-   **Offer:** Present a clear, user-initiated passkey action.
-   **Defer:** Suppress an opportunistic prompt because the context, frequency policy or available evidence does not justify it.
-   **Fallback:** Continue to an approved alternative when passkeys are unavailable, fail or cannot satisfy policy.

Capability support is necessary but does not prove that a usable credential exists or that the active credential provider can complete the flow. Decisions should preserve `supported`, `unsupported` and `unknown` states and describe credential availability as a likelihood rather than certainty.

## Inputs and boundaries

Inputs can include official client-capability signals, server-known credential history, prior first-party success or failure, account policy, rollout group and device-specific first-party state. They do not authenticate the user and must not enable cross-site tracking or account enumeration.

Creation nudges use one account-level history across post-login, signup and in-app entry points that target the same enrollment goal. Multiple nudges across separate eligible sessions are the default. Daily, monthly or yearly limits control the total frequency across those entry points.

The decision layer complements the individual flow criteria. It does not replace server-side WebAuthn verification, fallback, management or lifecycle reconciliation.
